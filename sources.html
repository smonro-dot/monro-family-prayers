Text sources

The prayers in this app are traditional or public-domain texts. The copyrighted English Liturgy of the Hours is not used.

Scripture

  • Psalms and the Canticle of Simeon (Luke 2:29–32) are the Douay-Rheims Bible, Challoner revision, the 1899 American edition, which is in the public domain. Psalm titles and footnotes are left out. Verse numbers follow that edition. Hebrew psalm numbers are shown beside the Douay-Rheims numbers.
  • The night reading is Jeremias 14:9 in the same Douay-Rheims text.

Night Prayer

  • The order is traditional Roman Compline: opening versicles, examination, Confiteor, hymn, psalm, chapter, responsory, Nunc dimittis, collect, blessing, and a Marian antiphon.
  • One psalm group is read each night, in the seven-night pattern (Sunday Psalm 90 through Saturday Psalms 4 and 133). The wording of the psalms is Douay-Rheims, not a modern psalter.
  • The English of the ordinary (opening, Confiteor, responsory, collect, blessing, and antiphons) is a traditional rendering of the public-domain Latin of the Roman Breviary. It is not the ICEL translation.
  • The hymn is the English of Te lucis ante terminum by J. M. Neale, public domain, as commonly printed in nineteenth-century hymnals.
  • Outside Eastertide the Marian antiphon is the traditional English Hail Holy Queen. In Eastertide the Regina Caeli is used instead. Both English texts are traditional renderings of the public-domain Latin.

Morning, Rosary, and common prayers

  • Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory Be, Apostles’ Creed, Hail Holy Queen, and the Angelus use long-standard English Catholic forms that are in the public domain.
  • The Morning Offering is the traditional English form of the Apostleship of Prayer, widely printed in Catholic prayer books of the early twentieth century.
  • The Act of Contrition is the form in the Baltimore Catechism (1885).
  • The Guardian Angel prayer is the traditional English rhyme.
  • The Saint Michael prayer is an English rendering of the 1886 Leonine prayer. The Latin is public domain.
  • Rosary instructions and the one-line mystery notes were written for this app. They name the Gospel events. They are not taken from a copyrighted rosary booklet.
  • The optional prayer after the Glory Be is a customary English form of the prayer taught at Fatima in 1917. It is marked optional because it is not part of the older rosary.
  • The Luminous Mysteries are the five Gospel scenes proposed in 2002. Only the names of the events are used.
