# Text sources

The app uses traditional or public-domain texts. It does not use the copyrighted English Liturgy of the Hours (ICEL) or a copyrighted psalter.

The same notes are on [sources.html](sources.html), which is cached for offline reading.

## Scripture

- Psalms, the Canticle of Simeon (Luke 2:29–32), and Jeremias 14:9 are the Douay-Rheims Bible, Challoner revision, 1899 American edition, public domain.
- Psalm titles (inscriptions) and Challoner footnotes are omitted. Verse numbers follow that edition.
- Headings also give the Hebrew psalm number, because many Bibles use it. The words are still the Douay-Rheims (Vulgate) numbering.

A convenient reading copy of this public-domain text is [drbo.org](https://www.drbo.org/). That site is not the copyright holder.

## Night Prayer

- Shape: traditional Roman Compline (opening versicles, examination, Confiteor, hymn, psalm, chapter, responsory, Nunc dimittis, collect, blessing, Marian antiphon).
- The seven nights use one psalm group each: Sunday 90, Monday 85, Tuesday 142, Wednesday 30 and 129, Thursday 15, Friday 87, Saturday 4 and 133 (Douay-Rheims numbers).
- English for the ordinary is a traditional rendering of the public-domain Latin of the Roman Breviary, not the ICEL text.
- Hymn: J. M. Neale’s public-domain English of *Te lucis ante terminum*, as commonly printed in nineteenth-century hymnals.
- Marian antiphon: traditional English Hail Holy Queen outside Eastertide; Regina Caeli in Eastertide.

## Other prayers

- Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory Be, Apostles’ Creed, Hail Holy Queen, and the Angelus: long-standard public-domain English.
- Morning Offering: traditional Apostleship of Prayer English form printed in early twentieth-century Catholic prayer books.
- Act of Contrition: Baltimore Catechism (1885).
- Guardian Angel: traditional English rhyme.
- Saint Michael: English rendering of the 1886 Leonine prayer (Latin public domain).
- Rosary steps and one-line mystery notes: written for this app from the Gospel events, not copied from a copyrighted booklet.
- The short prayer after the Glory Be is a customary English form of the 1917 Fatima prayer and is marked optional.
- Luminous Mysteries: names of the five Gospel scenes proposed in 2002. The papal document itself is not quoted.
